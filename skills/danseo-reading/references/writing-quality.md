# Writing and reading checks

Summary: Write for the reader's task, then read and revise the finished text. Preserve source claims, conditions and uncertainty. These checks travel with both skills and require no personal configuration.

## Before writing

Identify the reader, the question they need answered and the evidence available. Use the user's requested language; English instructions do not require English answers. Set standalone candidate `language` explicitly to `en` or `ko` for its fixed UI. Preserve source text rather than translating records automatically. Explain the English-UI fallback for other prose languages; there is no live language switch or translation service.

Lead with the useful content. Connect a claim to its reason, example or consequence instead of listing disconnected facts. Use familiar, precise words and visible subjects and actions. For Korean output, check literal translations, unnatural word order, long noun chains and unnecessary passive constructions. Keep technical identifiers and quotations exact.

## Read before handing off

Read the finished reply or view in its actual reading order. Check whether the main point is easy to find, the connections between paragraphs are clear and each detail helps the reader. Use lists for parallel items or sequences and tables for comparisons or exact mappings. Do not repeat the same point in an introduction, body and closing summary.

Remove empty emphasis, unnecessary process commentary and generic warnings that do not apply to the current record. When a relation has no linked source evidence, say so once; do not introduce nonexistent evidence as “the claims below.” Keep the distinction between an author claim, an AI interpretation, a project observation and an adoption decision.

Compare edited text with its source. Preserve facts, numbers, actors, dates, negation, conditions, limitations, citations, links, code, requirement strength and verification status. Never improve fluency by inventing evidence, removing an exception or presenting an assisted response as independent understanding. Exact learner attempts remain unchanged; put explanations or corrections beside them.

Revise the affected passage and read it again. Short labels and replies need this check too. Source schemas, successful generation and automated lint are useful checks, but do not establish readable prose or correct interpretation. Do not add a checklist or a “review complete” declaration to every user-facing answer.

## Generated views

Read the rendered titles, body, relation directions, evidence and notices at normal size. Inspect selection states as well as the initial view: readable unselected titles, continuous edges, restrained labels, clear focus and access to complete text. Keep relation numbers in the list or reader when an on-edge badge obscures a short route. Preserve the existing design and semantic status colors.

Apply the owning view's required viewport and interaction checks, recheck affected screens after a repair and state anything that could not be inspected. A screenshot, a valid schema or a click is not proof of factual truth, reproducibility, user understanding or design preference.
